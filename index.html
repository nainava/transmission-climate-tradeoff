Delayed: gas & coal backfill On time: clean corridor

Two futures for the same demand. While the line is late, gas and coal fill in; if the backfill decarbonizes on its own, the red line slopes down and each late year costs less. Once the line is online both paths are clean. The gap is the delay penalty, labelled in tonnes rather than left for the eye to measure.

How the numbers are built. Energy delivered = capacity (MW) × 8,760 h × capacity factor × (1 − line losses). Backfill emission factor = (1 − zero-carbon share) × [coal share × coal EF + (1 − coal share) × gas EF], falling by the decarbonization rate each year. Delay penalty = ∫ energy × (backfill EF(t) − residual EF) over the delay years; car-equivalents assume 4.6 t CO₂ per car-year. Sources for default values. Emission factors — EPA GHG Equivalencies & EIA (coal ≈2,180 lb, gas CCGT ≈890 lb CO₂/MWh). NW avoided-emissions rate ≈0.58 lb/kWh (≈0.29 t/MWh) — NW Power & Conservation Council (2018 report to the WA Legislature). Corridor scale, line length and timing — NWEC / Energy Strategies, Montana Regional Transmission Connectivity Study (Low +3 GW via a 432-mi Montana–Idaho HVDC line, ~$3.1B; Medium +9 GW, ~2,074 routed mi; High +12 GW, ~2,484 mi; low route 58% in existing corridors, 2% high environmental risk; North Plains Connector 3 GW, construction 2028 → in service 2032; the Montana–Idaho concept dates to the 2008 MSTI proposal and is still unbuilt). HVDC losses — Stanford PH240 & EIA HVDC study (~3% per 1,000 km on the line plus ~0.6–1% per converter station). Capacity factors — NREL 2024 ATB, DOE WINDExchange & MSU Montana Wind Status (MT fleet ~33%, high-quality sites 40%+; Columbia Basin ~28–32%). Passenger-vehicle CO₂ — EPA (4.6 t/yr).
